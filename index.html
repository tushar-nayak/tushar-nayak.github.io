Tushar Nayak

building geometry-aware vision systems for image-guided robotic surgery

I am a staff scientist at the Surgical Biomechanics Lab at the University of Chicago. I collaborate with the University of Pittsburgh's Surreality Lab and Carnegie Mellon University's Computational Engineering & Robotics Lab (CERLAB), where I am also an alumnus.

I build computer vision and learning systems that recover anatomy, motion, and deformation from sparse clinical data, with applications in medical imaging and image-guided robotic intervention.

Featured Projects

All projects GitHub
FluoroGRID: Real-Time 3D Vascular Deformation from Monocular FluoroscopyEstimates patient-specific 3D neurovascular deformation from a single fluoroscopic view by fusing image evidence, pre-operative anatomy, and camera geometry. Sparse-View Cardiac Reconstruction with Neural Implicit RepresentationsCompletes 3D left-ventricular geometry from three annotated echocardiography sections, scored only on the voxels the model never observed. Dual-View Mammogram Alignment & ClassificationAligns and fuses CC and MLO mammograms with spatial transformers, then predicts BI-RADS categories from both views. Patient-Specific Aortic MultiphysicsMaps patient-specific aortic geometry, longitudinal growth, and available hemodynamic fields, then tests how their relationships hold across regions and mesh scales.
