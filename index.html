Tushar Nayak

building geometry-aware vision systems for image-guided robotic surgery

Surgery Brain Research Pavilion, University of Chicago

I am a staff scientist at the Surgical Biomechanics Lab under Dr. Luka Pocivavsek at the University of Chicago, and a research collaborator with the University of Pittsburgh’s Surreality Lab. I recently completed my M.S. in Biomedical Engineering at Carnegie Mellon University.

I build computer vision and learning systems that recover useful anatomy, motion, and deformation from sparse, noisy, clinically constrained data, spanning geometric vision, 3D reconstruction, and physics-informed deep learning for image-guided intervention.

I am currently applying to PhD programs. My interests are in sparse-view 2D–3D reconstruction, deformable anatomical modeling and registration, neural implicit and Gaussian representations, graph-based geometric learning, and physics-informed methods for image-guided intervention. More on my research interests and project work, or see my CV for the complete record.

featured projects

  • FluoroGRID: Real-Time 3D Vascular Deformation from Monocular Fluoroscopy
    Estimates patient-specific 3D neurovascular deformation from a single fluoroscopic view by fusing image evidence, pre-operative anatomy, and camera geometry.
  • Open-Vocabulary Surgical Tool Detection and Tracking
    Detects and tracks surgical instruments from free-text prompts, removing the fixed-label assumption that limits closed-set surgical perception.
  • Sparse-View Cardiac Reconstruction with Neural Implicit Representations
    Completes 3D left-ventricular geometry from three annotated echocardiography sections, scored only on the voxels the model never observed.
